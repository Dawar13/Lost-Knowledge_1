\begin{abstract}
Language models are said to collapse onto common answers and lose the long tail of knowledge. We separate three cases: knowledge a model lacks, knowledge it holds but rarely says, and knowledge it holds but cannot say at all because the sampler gives it probability exactly zero. Top-p, top-k and min-p sampling delete low-ranked tokens before sampling, so a name loses all its probability if any one of its tokens is deleted. We give an exact, sampling-free test of this, one forward pass per answer, with a numeric margin that makes the verdict robust to floating-point differences between implementations. On \gzEntities{} long-tail entities, \gzQwenInstPnine{} of the entities that Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct verifies (yes/no and four-way choice against near-miss foils) can never be produced under top-p 0.9, and \gzQwenInstShipped{} under its shipped sampler, against \gzQwenBasePnine{} for its base model. Across the four post-training stages of OLMo-2 the share rises from \goRbuOlmoBase{} to \goRbuOlmoInst{}, most of it at supervised fine-tuning, and the cut moves to the first token of the name. Any estimate of model knowledge built from samples therefore measures the sampler, and we compute the gap exactly. On \ptCategories{} categories from 24 countries the rare entities are the ones deleted: each tenfold increase in an entity's Wikipedia coverage multiplies its odds of being unreachable by \ptOrUnreachQwenInst{}. From samples under the shipped sampler, Chao1 estimates \peQwenShippedChao{} entities where the model recognises \peQwenShippedRecog{}. Switching truncation off at the first token recovers only \pfRecoveredQwen{} of the unreachable entities, because names fail again later, so we report the frontier between reachable knowledge and invented answers instead.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Ask a chat model to name a poet who wrote in Tamil and it will usually say Thiruvalluvar. Ask it whether \emph{Kumaragurupara Desikar} was a Tamil poet and it will usually say yes. Both behaviours are expected. The question this paper asks is different: can the model say \emph{Kumaragurupara Desikar} at all when asked for a Tamil poet, however many times we ask?

Under the sampler the model ships with, often it cannot. Nucleus (top-p), top-k and min-p sampling keep only the most likely tokens at each step and delete the rest before sampling. If one token of a name falls outside the kept set at any step, the probability of the whole name is exactly zero. No number of samples, retries or self-consistency votes can produce it.

This matters for the current debate on homogenisation and knowledge collapse. When a model never produces a rare fact, three different things can be true: the model does not store it; it stores it but gives it a small probability, so enough samples will find it; or it stores it and the sampler deletes it. The third case looks exactly like the first from samples, but it is a property of a decoding setting, not of the model's knowledge.

We make the third case measurable. Our contributions:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Recognised-but-unreachable knowledge.} We pair an exact reachability test (one teacher-forced pass per answer, also used for ordinary words by \citealp{awad2026lost}) with a recognition test against near-miss foils, and add a numeric margin so the verdict does not depend on floating-point differences between two computations of the same logits. Real samples from two model families never contradict it (Section~\ref{sec:method}).
\item \textbf{How much.} Among entities Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct recognises, \gzQwenInstShipped{} of those in our pilot are unreachable under its shipped sampler (Section~\ref{sec:howmuch}).
\item \textbf{Where it comes from.} Across OLMo-2's post-training stages RBU rises mostly at supervised fine-tuning, the cut moves to the first token of the name, and first-token concentration tracks RBU; truncation by probability mass, not by rank, does the deleting (Section~\ref{sec:where}).
\item \textbf{What it does to measurement.} Unseen-species estimators applied to model samples converge to the reachable set, not to what the model recognises; we compute the gap exactly (Section~\ref{sec:measure}).
\item \textbf{Who loses, and what a fix can and cannot do.} Rare entities are deleted more often (Section~\ref{sec:who}). Relaxing truncation at the first token alone recovers little, because names fail again later; we report the full frontier between reachable knowledge and invented answers instead (Section~\ref{sec:fix}).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Exact reachability}\label{sec:method}

\paragraph{Samplers.} At each decoding step a sampler applies, in the order used by Hugging Face \texttt{transformers}: repetition penalty, temperature $T$, top-$k$, top-$p$ and min-$p$. Each step sets some logits to $-\infty$; sampling draws from the softmax of the rest. A token is \emph{kept} if its processed logit is finite.

\paragraph{Keep rules.} For a target token $t$ with logit $z_t$: top-$k$ keeps $t$ iff fewer than $k$ tokens have a larger logit; top-$p$ keeps $t$ iff $G(t)<p$, where $G(t)$ is the probability, at temperature $T$, of the tokens strictly more likely than $t$; min-$p$ keeps $t$ iff its probability is at least $\textit{min-p}$ times the top token's. We prove these rules equal the library's processors in unit tests on random logits.

\paragraph{Reachability of an answer.} Write an entity $e$ after prompt $x$ as tokens $t_1\ldots t_L$ (the tokenisation the model itself produces in context). $e$ is reachable under sampler $S$ iff every $t_j$ is kept given $x$ and $t_{<j}$. One teacher-forced pass gives every $t_j$'s logits, so reachability is exact and needs no sampling. It also yields critical values: $p^*(e)=\max_j G_j(e)$ ($e$ is reachable under top-$p$ iff $p>p^*$), $k^*(e)$ and the critical min-$p$. For combined samplers (the shipped settings) we run the library's own processor chain at every position. An entity is reachable if any of its surface forms (label or up to three aliases) is.

\paragraph{A numeric margin.} The same logits computed two ways (a batched teacher-forced pass, and key-value-cached generation) differ slightly in bfloat16, and bfloat16 logits often tie. A token on a truncation boundary can therefore be kept by one computation and cut by the other. We call $e$ \emph{robustly unreachable} if some token stays cut even after its logit is raised by $\epsilon$ relative to every other token. Every processor above is monotone in this shift, and the shift breaks all ties. $\epsilon$ is calibrated per model before each experiment: we sample about two thousand answers from the model, compute for each the logit shift at which its verdict would flip, and take the smallest value on a fixed grid that covers every sampled answer (Appendix~\ref{app:margin}). For Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, $\epsilon=\gzEps$; the largest relative logit gap among the tokens that decide a verdict was \gzGapMax{} logits. Headline numbers use the robust verdict, a lower bound; exact and upper-bound values are reported beside them (Appendix~
ef{app:margin}).

\paragraph{Validation.} We draw 1{,}000 real samples per category from \texttt{generate()} under each claimed sampler and check that no answer we call robustly unreachable is ever produced. Across \gzSamples{} samples for Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct and \goSamples{} for the OLMo-2 post-trained stages there were \gzViolations{} and \goViolations{} violations. On CPU in float32 the two computations agree exactly and every sampled answer is scored reachable.

\section{TailCat and recognition}\label{sec:data}

\paragraph{Categories.} TailCat pairs categories of long-tail entities with near-miss foils from sibling categories, built from Wikidata: for example poets who wrote in Tamil (foils: Telugu and Kannada poets) or rivers in Kerala (foils: rivers in Karnataka and Tamil Nadu). Members are stratified over popularity (Wikipedia sitelinks) and must have articles in at least two languages, which removes single-wiki bot imports whose transliterated labels no reader could know. The pilot has 11 categories (\gzEntities{} entities) including a head control, US state capitals. The full TailCat crosses 14 entity types (people, places, works and organisations) with 24 countries, 17 of them in South Asia, Africa, Southeast Asia and Latin America, and adds writers in 12 Indian languages. Foils come from the same type in neighbouring or easily confused countries. We keep a category only if it yields at least 30 members and 30 foils: \ptCategories{} categories and \ptMembers{} sampled members (at most 150 per category).

\paragraph{Recognition.} An entity counts as recognised if the model (i) answers ``Yes'' to `Is ``$e$'' \emph{category}?' with a score $V(e)=P(\text{Yes})/(P(\text{Yes})+P(\text{No}))$ at or above the 95th percentile of the category's foils, so at most 5\% of foils pass, and (ii) picks $e$ out of three foils in a four-way choice. RBU is the share of recognised entities that are unreachable. The yes/no probe separates members from foils with AUROC \gzQwenInstAuroc{} for Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct on the pilot and \ptAurocQwenInst{} on the full TailCat, below the 0.85 we set in advance; the main results hold on the categories where it exceeds 0.85 on its own, and under a second probe wording (Appendix~\ref{app:recognition}).

\section{How much recognised knowledge is unreachable?}\label{sec:howmuch}

Table~\ref{tab:main} reports RBU for the two families. Under the sampler Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct ships with (temperature 0.7, top-$k$ 20, top-$p$ 0.8, repetition penalty 1.05) \gzQwenInstShipped{} of the recognised pilot entities are unreachable (95\% cluster-bootstrap CI \gzQwenInstShippedLo{} to \gzQwenInstShippedHi{}). Under plain top-$p$ 0.9 it is \gzQwenInstPnine{} (\gzQwenInstPnineLo{} to \gzQwenInstPnineHi{}), against \gzQwenBasePnine{} for the base model. On the full TailCat the numbers are \ptRbuQwenInst{} (\ptRbuQwenInstLo{} to \ptRbuQwenInstHi{}) for Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct and \ptRbuOlmoInst{} for OLMo-2-Instruct, against \ptRbuQwenBase{} and \ptRbuOlmoBase{} for their base models.

\paragraph{The effect is not a quirk of wording or temperature.} Across five prompt wordings the coefficient of variation of RBU at top-$p$ 0.9 is \rbCvQwenInst{} for Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct and \rbCvOlmoInst{} for OLMo-2-Instruct. Lowering the temperature raises RBU: for the Qwen base model from \rbTempQwenBaseTone{} at $T=1$ to \rbTempQwenBaseTseven{} at $T=0.7$ and \rbTempQwenBaseTfive{} at $T=0.5$. Counting only the canonical label, not aliases, gives \rbLabelOnlyQwenInst{} against \rbTempQwenInstTone{} with aliases.

\paragraph{Scale does not remove it.} Across Qwen2.5-Instruct at 0.5B, 1.5B, 3B and 7B parameters the number of recognised pilot entities grows (\scRecognisedHalf{}, \scRecognisedOneHalf{}, \scRecognisedThree{} and \gzQwenInstRecognised{}), and the share unreachable under each model's shipped sampler stays high (\scShippedHalf{}, \scShippedOneHalf{}, \scShippedThree{} and \gzQwenInstShipped{}). The smallest model's recognition test is weak (AUROC \scAurocHalf{}), so its value is the least reliable.

\paragraph{Even head knowledge collapses.} The head control, US state capitals, was meant to be reachable by every model. The base model reaches all \gzCapitalsMembers{} capitals in our pilot list under top-$p$ 0.9; the instruct model reaches \gzCapitalsReachInst{}.

\section{Where it comes from}\label{sec:where}

\paragraph{Post-training, stage by stage.} OLMo-2 releases its base model and each post-training stage. RBU at top-$p$ 0.9 is \goRbuOlmoBase{} for the base model, \goRbuOlmoSft{} after supervised fine-tuning, \goRbuOlmoDpo{} after preference tuning and \goRbuOlmoInst{} for the final model. Most of the rise happens at supervised fine-tuning. Among entities recognised by both, \goPairedInstOnly{} are unreachable only for the final model and \goPairedBaseOnly{} only for the base model; for Qwen2.5 the counts are \gzPairedInstOnly{} and \gzPairedBaseOnly{} (sign tests far below $p=0.01$). Neither fine-tuning objective can set a probability to zero by itself: the KL-regularised preference optimum keeps the support of the reference model, and cross-entropy training keeps logits finite. What they change is where the mass goes.

\paragraph{The first token.} The share of unreachable recognised entities whose first failing token is the first token of the name rises from \goFirstTokOlmoBase{} (OLMo-2 base) to \goFirstTokOlmoInst{} (OLMo-2-Instruct), and from \gzQwenBaseFirstTok{} to \gzQwenInstFirstTok{} for Qwen2.5. The mean probability of the single most likely first answer token rises with it, from \goTopOneOlmoBase{} to \goTopOneOlmoInst{} (OLMo-2) and from \goTopOneQwenBase{} to \goTopOneQwenInst{} (Qwen2.5). Across \goSpearmanN{} (model, category) points, first-token concentration and RBU have Spearman correlation \goSpearman{}. Appendix~\ref{app:walk} shows real names token by token: the instruct model rules them out at the first token, while the base model starts them and loses them later.

\paragraph{Mass, not rank.} Top-$k$ RBU barely moves across OLMo-2's stages (\goKfiftyOlmoBase{} to \goKfiftyOlmoInst{} at $k=50$) while top-$p$ RBU rises steeply. Post-training changes how much probability the head takes far more than the order of the candidates, so truncation by mass (top-$p$, min-$p$) is what deletes the tail.

\section{What it does to measurement}\label{sec:measure}

Let $q_S(e)$ be the probability that one sample under sampler $S$ is entity $e$. After $N$ samples the expected number of distinct entities seen is $\sum_e[1-(1-q_S(e))^N]$, which rises to the size of the reachable set $\{e:q_S(e)>0\}$. Every unseen-species estimator, from Good-Turing to Chao1 and ACE, estimates the size of that support. Applied to truncated samples it estimates the reachable set, never the recognised set, and the shortfall is $\mathrm{RBU}(S)\times|\text{recognised}|$, which our test computes exactly. \citet{li2025unseen} observed that nucleus sampling lowers their knowledge estimates and sampled without it; the argument above explains why and quantifies it.

Empirically, on the full TailCat with 1{,}000 samples per category under Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct's shipped sampler, the samples name \peQwenShippedSobs{} distinct members; Chao1 estimates \peQwenShippedChao{} and ACE \peQwenShippedAce{}, while \peQwenShippedReach{} members are reachable and the model recognises \peQwenShippedRecog{}. Only \peQwenShippedOutside{} sampled members lie outside the computed reachable set, each produced through a non-canonical token path (Limitations); no sample matched the token sequence of a robustly unreachable answer. Under top-$p$ 0.9 the corresponding numbers are \peQwenPnineSobs{}, \peQwenPnineChao{}, \peQwenPnineReach{} and \peQwenPnineRecog{}.

\section{Who loses}\label{sec:who}

\paragraph{Popularity.} We fit a logistic model of unreachability on $\log_{10}(1+\text{sitelinks})$ for recognised entities, with generalised estimating equations clustered by category \citep{liang1986longitudinal}. For Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct each tenfold increase in Wikipedia language coverage multiplies the odds of being unreachable by \ptOrUnreachQwenInst{} (95\% CI \ptOrUnreachQwenInstLo{} to \ptOrUnreachQwenInstHi{}); for OLMo-2-Instruct by \ptOrUnreachOlmoInst{}. The same model for recognition among all members gives \ptOrRecogQwenInst{} for Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct.

\paragraph{Regions and types.} Appendix~\ref{app:breakdowns} breaks RBU down by world region and entity type. RBU is not higher for categories from South Asia, Africa or Southeast Asia than for European or North American ones; it is lower. A likely reason, which we have not tested, is composition: the instruct model recognises more members of Western categories, and a category's many recognised members compete for the same few first tokens, so a larger share of them is cut. The robust pattern is within categories: the rarer a recognised entity, the more likely it is unreachable.

\section{A targeted fix}\label{sec:fix}

If most failures happen at the first token, a natural fix is to switch truncation off for the first answer token only and keep the shipped sampler afterwards. We tested this against a sweep of standard samplers on the pilot (1{,}000 samples per category per sampler), measuring RBU, the number of distinct real members produced and the invented-name rate (answers that match no member of the category in Wikidata and no foil).

The fix fails our pre-registered bar of recovering half of the unreachable recognised entities. It recovers \pfRecoveredQwen{} for Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct (RBU from \pfQwenRefRbu{} to \pfQwenRelaxRbu{}, distinct members sampled from \pfQwenRefDistinct{} to \pfQwenRelaxDistinct{}, invented-name rate \pfInvDeltaQwen{} points) and \pfRecoveredOlmo{} for OLMo-2-Instruct (invented-name rate $+$\pfInvDeltaOlmo{} points). The reason is instructive. The first failing token is usually the first token, but most names fail again later under the same sampler, so relaxing one position rescues only names whose every failure is at the start.

The full frontier is the more useful result (Appendix~\ref{app:frontier}). Removing truncation entirely makes every recognised entity reachable at a cost of \pfQwenUntrInv{} invented answers against \pfQwenRefInv{} under the shipped sampler for Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct; top-$p$ 0.99, top-$k$ 100 and epsilon sampling lie in between. The automatic invented-name rate overstates invention: it counts real members spelled differently or missing from Wikidata, and wrong answers, as invented. With looser matching the rates fall by about ten points while the differences between samplers barely change (relaxation: $+$\pfLooseDeltaQwen{} and $+$\pfLooseDeltaOlmo{} points). A hand audit of 200 such answers is pending.

\section{Related work}

\paragraph{Truncation and what it removes.} Nucleus sampling \citep{holtzman2020curious}, its desmoothing view with epsilon and eta sampling \citep{hewitt2022truncation} and min-$p$ \citep{nguyen2025minp} all trade the tail of the distribution for fluency. \citet{lee2022factuality} show top-$p$ can harm factuality and adapt it. The closest measurement is \citet{awad2026lost}, who use the same token-survival test to ask which \emph{words} of book continuations remain reachable, compare base and aligned models and tabulate shipped defaults; \citet{garcesarias2026truncation} show that human-chosen tokens often fall outside common truncation sets. We ask a different question: whether \emph{knowledge the model demonstrably recognises} is reachable, why post-training changes that (stage by stage, through the first answer token), and what follows for knowledge estimates. We also make the verdict robust to numeric differences between implementations, which the per-token test needs at truncation boundaries.

\paragraph{Knowing without saying.} \citet{gekhman2025insideout} find that a model can rank the gold answer first internally and never generate it in 1{,}000 samples drawn at temperature 1, without analysing truncation; \citet{calderon2026empty} find that recall, not encoding, is the bottleneck for parametric factuality. Long-tail knowledge is harder to learn \citep{kandpal2023longtail} and to trust \citep{mallen2023trust}; a recent survey names decoding-time truncation as a possible cause without measuring it \citep{badhe2026longtail}. Our case is the zero-probability one: no amount of sampling helps.

\paragraph{Homogenisation and post-training.} Post-training reduces output diversity \citep{kirk2024understanding}; open-ended answers converge within and across models \citep{jiang2025hivemind} and across languages \citep{davey2026multilingual}; diversity collapses at identifiable post-training stages \citep{karouzos2026diversity}; and prompting can recover suppressed diversity \citep{zhang2026verbalized}. \citet{kalavasis2025limits} prove a trade-off between breadth and hallucination. These works measure diversity at a fixed decoding setting; \citet{jiang2025hivemind} find homogeneity persists under min-$p$. We separate the part of collapse that is a decoding artefact, exactly.

\paragraph{Estimating what a model knows from samples.} \citet{li2025unseen} estimate unseen knowledge with smoothed Good-Turing and observe that nucleus sampling lowers their estimates, so they sample without it. We show why any such estimator converges to the reachable set under truncation and compute the gap exactly, using Chao1 \citep{chao1984nonparametric} and ACE \citep{chao1992coverage} as examples.

\section*{Limitations}

\paragraph{Recognition.} Our recognition test is a proxy. Its yes/no part separates members from near-miss foils with AUROC around 0.8, below the 0.85 we set in advance; chat models answer ``Yes'' readily to plausible foils. The four-way choice and the per-category foil threshold limit false positives, and the main results hold on the categories that pass on their own and under a second probe wording, but RBU is defined relative to this test.

\paragraph{Tokenisation.} We test the tokenisation the model itself produces in context. A name can sometimes be produced through another token path. In the full-TailCat sample check of Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, only \ptNoncanonEntShipped{} distinct entities (shipped sampler) and \ptNoncanonEntPnine{} (top-$p$ 0.9) were ever produced solely through a non-canonical path, so canonical scoring overstates RBU by a negligible amount.

\paragraph{Numerics.} Exact zero depends on how logits are computed. Our robust verdict holds for any computation within a calibrated margin of ours; implementations further away (other GPUs, quantisation) need their own calibration.

\paragraph{Scope.} Two model families at 7B scale, English prompts and Wikidata categories. Llama-3.1 and Gemma-2 were not run, and the multilingual analysis planned for Hindi, Tamil and Bengali prompts is future work. Wikidata membership is incomplete, which affects the invented-name rate more than RBU.

\section{Numeric margin}\label{app:margin}
For each model we sample 500 answers per pilot category with a separate seed, compute for each distinct answer the logit shift at which its reachability verdict flips (by bisection), and set $\epsilon$ to the smallest value on the grid $\{0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 1, 1.5, 2, 3, 4, 6\}$ that covers every sampled answer. For Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, \gzCalSeqs{} distinct sampled answers gave \gzCalExact{} exact-verdict contradictions and none at $\epsilon=\gzEps$; the 99.9th percentile of the relevant logit gap was \gzGapQnine{}. In the Qwen sample check, \gzExactViolations{} sampled answer would have contradicted the exact verdict and none contradicted the robust one.
